# 12. Epilogue

Dee brought the pie.

Two slices, two plates. She set them between the laptop and the last clean patch of table, then refilled Steve's coffee.

"You two settled it?"

Kevin looked at Steve. Then he laughed, a proper laugh this time, loud enough that the man watching the Cowboys turned around.

"I think we did."

He pulled his phone out and typed a message to Dale. Read it. Deleted the last sentence. Typed it again.

"What'd you tell him?" Steve asked.

"That I have a plan. And I can explain why this one should work."

He put the phone down and picked up his sweet tea.

"That is worth a drink."

Steve lifted his coffee. They touched the glass and the cup together carefully, and Kevin took a long swallow.

"You know what I was going to do before I called you?"

"Add another thread."

"Add another thread."

Steve took a bite of pie.

"You called."

Kevin shook his head, still grinning. He folded napkin ten once and slipped it into his laptop bag.

"Friday morning," he said. "I'm going to walk Dale through it Friday morning."

"Run your checks first."

"I know. I want to go into that room with something I can show him."

For a while they ate. On the TV, a Dallas linebacker chased a quarterback out of bounds. Somebody at the bar had an opinion about the spot.

Dee brought the check. Kevin reached for it, but Steve put his hand over it.

"I've got this."

"I dragged you out here."

"And now you owe me a working demo."

Steve paid the bill and left a hundred-dollar tip.

Dee looked at the money, then at the spread of paper beside his plate.

"That for the food or the office space?"

"Napkin budget."

"You're still short."

She leaned across to collect the plates. Steve glanced at her nametag.

**DAWN.**

He smiled. "Dawn. Would you have dinner with me?"

She stopped with a plate in each hand.

"Somewhere else?"

"Anywhere you like."

"And that stays home." She nodded at the laptop.

Kevin looked from one to the other. "Hang on."

Steve waited.

"You know her?"

"I'm married to her."

Kevin put his fork down.

"I've been sitting here talking about gas pumps with your wife bringing us tea?"

"Bringing you tea," Dawn said. "He drinks coffee."

"You could have told me."

"You were having a good night," Steve said.

Dawn tucked the check into her apron.

"Friday. Seven. I'm picking the place."

Steve nodded and zipped his laptop bag closed.
